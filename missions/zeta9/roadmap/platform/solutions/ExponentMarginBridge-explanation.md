Let $\tau=2641/250$. Suppose an eventual upper bound $B(2k+2)\le\beta<\tau$ holds and $\sigma(2k+2)\to0$ as $k\to\infty$. Then there is a fixed $\varepsilon>0$ such that, eventually,

$$
B(2k+2)+\sigma(2k+2)\le\tau-\varepsilon.
$$

Take $\varepsilon=(\tau-\beta)/2>0$. Convergence to zero gives $\sigma(2k+2)<\varepsilon$ for every sufficiently large $k$. Intersecting this eventual statement with the eventual bound on $B$ yields

$$
B(2k+2)+\sigma(2k+2)<\beta+\varepsilon=\tau-\varepsilon.
$$

Weakening the strict inequality gives the claimed non-strict bound. This abstract step uses only the eventual bound and convergence hypotheses; it makes no assertion that the specific arithmetic volume or lattice-shape hypotheses for $\zeta(9)$ have been established.
