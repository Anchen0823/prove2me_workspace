**Resolution of the flagged asymmetry.** It is intended, and it is sufficient.
If two forms $b_i+a_i\,\zeta(9)$ both vanished, then both coefficient vectors
$(b_i,a_i)$ would lie in the span of $(-x,1)$ (with $x=\zeta(9)$; when $x=0$ a
vanishing form has $b_i=0$ and the vector is again $(0,a_i)$ in that span), so
$b_1a_2-b_2a_1=0$, contradicting the independence clause. Hence independence alone
already forces at least one form to be nonzero, which is exactly the hypothesis the
two-form criterion consumes. Requiring nonvanishing *in addition* would only
duplicate a consequence. The one-form obligation does need the clause explicitly,
because it has no independence assumption. This is now also stated in
`ExponentialTwoFormObligation-statement.md`.
